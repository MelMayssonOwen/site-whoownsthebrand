What is a trademark assignment?

A trademark assignment is the legal transfer of a trademark’s ownership from one entity (the assignor) to another (the assignee). In the United States these transfers are recorded with the USPTO and are public. That makes the assignment record the single most useful paper trail for finding out who really owns a brand, it shows the LLC on the label being handed up to its corporate parent, with dates.

Why assignments matter for ownership research

When a big company buys a brand, the brand’s trademark has to move to the buyer’s chosen legal entity. That move is an assignment. Because it is recorded and dated, you can reconstruct exactly when a brand changed hands, often long before, or after, the deal made headlines. Marketing may still present a brand as independent; the assignment record does not.

How to read an assignment record

  • Assignor / assignee. Who transferred the mark, and who received it. The assignee is the new owner.
  • Execution date vs recording date. When the parties signed vs when the USPTO recorded it. Use these to place ownership precisely in time.
  • Conveyance type. A full assignment, a merger, a change of name, or a security interest, each means something different for ownership.
  • Chain. Multiple assignments stack into a chain of title. Follow it to the current owner, then resolve that entity’s parent.

Search all of this for free in the USPTO Assignment Center. For the underlying registration and its current owner, use TSDR.

The LLC-on-the-record trap

Trademarks are frequently held by subsidiary or holding entities with generic names. Seeing an unfamiliar LLC as the owner does not mean a brand is independent, it usually means you have found only the first link. Cross-reference the entity in OpenCorporates and the parent’s SEC EDGAR filings to reach the ultimate parent. Our full method guide walks the whole chain.

FAQ

What is a trademark assignment?

A trademark assignment is the legal transfer of ownership of a trademark from one party (the assignor) to another (the assignee). In the U.S., assignments are recorded with the USPTO and are public, which is why they are the paper trail for tracing brand acquisitions.

How do I look up a trademark assignment?

Use the USPTO Assignment Center. Search by the trademark, registration number, or party name to see the chain of recorded assignments, including the entities involved and the execution and recording dates.

Does an assignment have to be recorded?

Recording is not strictly mandatory for an assignment to be valid between the parties, but recording gives public notice and protects the new owner. Because most real transfers are recorded, the Assignment Center is a reliable ownership trail.
